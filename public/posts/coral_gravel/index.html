Experimental study on swash zone transport of a coral-gravel beach

July 17, 2025

A coral gravel beach accumulated on top of a coral reef called Ballast Island in Okinawa, Japan (Taken from Figure 1 of Tajima (2018)).

Coral reefs and rocky coasts are present all over the globe. Large waves typically generated by storms are able to damage reefs and produce coralline sediments, wherein the reef acts as a sediment source for coral sediment. These reef fragments are then transported around on top of the reef by the waves. Generally, coral sediments are of sedimentary composition e.g. limestone with a porous nature. This porous nature of the sediment further contributes to the overall porosity of the overlying beach, in addition to the gaps in between sediment grains.

Coral gravel beaches are observed in reefs experiencing strong wave climate e.g. due to frequent storms. Gravels having a high overall porosity allow for steep acretionary slopes. On the other hand, corals have unique shapes which are not necessarily geometrically symmetric. Some shapes can also be angular and not curves as typical gravel. From this observation we can conclude that coral particles may have irregular shapes: angular and elongated, which is generally opposes the typical assumption for the variety of sediment models which work for sandy beaches.

We examine the case of a coral gravel beach near Iriomote island, Okinawa Prefecture, Japan, wherein accumulated sediment forms on top of the reef. These sediments are composed of long and sharp sediments, as well as large and round ones of about 10 cm in size on average. Ballast island is situated in between two other islands, Hatoma island and Iriomote island. The island group is frequented by storms which move the sediment around. Several factors can affect the movement of the sediment which include storm direction, location, strength, etc.

Due to the location of the beach on top of the reef flat, transport processes generally occur in the swash zone. We perform laboratory experiments of waves acting on a coral gravel beach in order to observe swash zone transport of the coral gravel beach.

Experimental setup (Taken from Figure 2 of Reyes and Tajima (2024)).

The experimental results showed that infiltration had a strong influence on reduction of offshore transport of sediment. For long wave cases, infiltration was observed to occur in the rundown as well. Qualitatively, exfiltration was observed both during the rundown in the upper swash and prior to the next runup, and exfiltration enhanced the movement of coral gravels in the offshore-ward and onshore-ward directions, respectively. Long coral particles were observed to dominate the steeper upper swash region, while round particles tend to fall into the lower swash during wave rundown. More information on the experiment and results can be found in my Master’s thesis.

The experiments make use of particle tracking velocimetry (PTV) to quantify groundwater flow inside the coral gravel bed, as well as image processing to obtain the swash zone water surface and bed profile. More information on the particle tracking can be found in Reyes and Tajima (2024).
