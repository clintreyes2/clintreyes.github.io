Tropical cylcones or storms with very strong winds can be largely destructive to the coastal communities. In addition to the presence of strong winds under a tropical cyclone is a surge or a sudden increase in the water level. On top of that there are also typically larger waves. This creates a very complex combination of physical processes interacting with each other.  
<!-- All these factors can contribute to coastal flooding or inundation.  -->

In addition, continuous exposure to the harsh wave climate of strong typhoons could lead to fragmentation of corals, which is another interesting topic. 
On the other hand, it is known that storms bring upon a surge of cooler water which can supplement nutrients to corals. 
Hence, there can be pros and cons to a tropical cyclone, and for such accurate modeling and understanding of the processes that occur during such is necessary. 

In this study we focus on wave modeling and wave generation due to wind, looking into the use of different parameterizations in SWAN. 
Essentially, the third generation (GEN3) wave action conservation model primarily considers a combination of two sources terms: (1) input from wind and (2) dissipation due to whitecapping. 
Wave generation due to wind is parameterized with a linear relationship, continously growing waves with windspeed. However, when waves reach a certain steepness, the waves must break and hence dissipate wave energy with the wave breaking. In deep water, wave breaking is also called whitecapping.
